What it is

Ask an AI whether your plan is good and it will tell you your plan is good. It was trained to be agreeable, and agreeable is worthless at the exact moment a decision matters. What you need at that moment is a room that pushes back.

Council makes your AI convene a bench of ten adversarial advisors on every major decision. Each one holds a single lens and argues it without mercy. Every decision produces a permanent record: what was decided, what the advisors argued, what was consciously accepted as risk. Six months later you don't wonder why you priced it that way. You read the record.

I run every significant Flowity decision through Council: pricing, architecture, go or no go. It has killed ideas I was in love with, and it has the receipts for the ones it approved.

The lineage

The idea of examining a decision through one deliberate lens at a time is not mine. It belongs to Edward de Bono, the Maltese physician and psychologist who published Six Thinking Hats in 1985 and called the method parallel thinking: instead of everyone arguing at once, the whole room looks in one direction at a time. Council keeps his insight and rebuilds it for AI. The hats become advisors engineered to genuinely disagree with you, and every session ends in a written verdict instead of a feeling.

The bench · ten seats

The original five interrogate the decision itself. The five new seats plug the bench into the rest of the stack: your memory, your documents, your market, your numbers, your downside.

  • The Contrarian Builds the strongest case against. What are we assuming that might be wrong?
  • The First Principles Thinker If we started from zero, would we design it this way?
  • The Expansionist What becomes possible if this succeeds? What are we not seeing?
  • The Outsider Reacts with no context and no politeness. What would a smart stranger think?
  • The Executor Reduces everything to the single next physical action.
  • The Historian Searches your decision records and memory for precedent. You tried something like this in March; here is how it went. Draws on Continuum.
  • The Librarian Argues only from what your documents actually say, receipts included. Draws on Canon.
  • The Signal Reader Brings the outside voices into the room: customers, reviews, market noise. Draws on Compass.
  • The Economist Cash, margin, unit economics, pricing power. If the numbers don't survive, the idea doesn't either.
  • The Guardian Maps the worst case and separates the reversible from the irreversible. You can walk through one way doors only once.
