Council — a decision engine

Your AI agrees with everything you say. That's a bug.

What it is

Ask an AI whether your plan is good and it will tell you your plan is good. It was trained to be agreeable, and agreeable is worthless at the exact moment a decision matters. What you need at that moment is a room that pushes back.

Council makes your AI convene five adversarial advisors on every major decision: a Contrarian who builds the strongest case against, a First-Principles thinker who asks if you'd design it this way from zero, an Expansionist who maps what you're not seeing, an Outsider who reacts with no context and no politeness, and an Executor who reduces it all to the single next physical action.

Every decision produces a permanent record: what was decided, what the advisors argued, what was consciously accepted as risk. Six months later you don't wonder why you priced it that way — you read the record.

I run every significant Flowity decision through Council — pricing, architecture, go/no-go. It has killed ideas I was in love with, and it has the receipts for the ones it approved.

What you get
  • The five-advisor framework, installed as a standing rule your AI applies to major decisions
  • Advisor prompts engineered to disagree with you, not perform disagreement
  • A decision-record template: verdict, dissent, accepted risk, next action
  • A permanent, searchable decision log that lives in your repo
  • Full documentation: when to convene, when to skip, how to extend the bench
