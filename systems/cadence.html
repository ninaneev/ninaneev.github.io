Cadence · Stage 05 · it executes

Systems don't fail loudly. They fail quietly, on a Tuesday. Cadence keeps them alive.

What it is

Every productivity system dies the same death: not from a flaw, but from a skipped week. Memory stops being pruned, documents stop being indexed, decisions stop being recorded, and three months later you're back to improvising. The missing piece was never another tool. It was rhythm.

Cadence gives your AI a working calendar. Each day opens with a plan built from your actual state: what memory says is in flight, what the signal brief flagged, what decisions are pending. Each week closes with a review that feeds everything back into the stack. Weekdays and weekends behave differently, because your business does.

It is the housekeeping layer for the other four systems: it keeps Compass scanning, Canon indexed, Continuum pruned, and Council convened when a decision deserves it. The stack stops depending on your discipline, which is exactly when it starts compounding.

What you get
  • A daily plan routine that opens from real state, not a blank page: three priorities max, each with one physical next action
  • A weekly review that prunes memory, refreshes indexes, and closes decision loops
  • Day aware behavior: workdays run the full engine, production and rest days protect recovery
  • A rollover wall: anything postponed twice gets rescheduled, killed, or escalated, never silently carried
  • Maintenance triggers for the other four systems, so nothing rots silently
  • Full documentation: fitting the rhythm to your week, not the other way around
Real examples
  • A founder's week: outbound days and ship days behave differently, and the day plan pulls from the pipeline, not a blank page
  • Exam prep: a study rhythm with a rollover wall, so the chapter you dodged twice gets rescheduled or dropped, never silently carried
  • A training block where rest days are protected by the system instead of guilt
  • A creator's production days open with the week's signal brief already on the desk
  • A team of one: the weekly review prunes memory, refreshes indexes, and closes decision loops before they rot
