I post on my LinkedIn, the Flowity page, X, a newsletter, Flowity's site and this blog. Alone. The only reason it works is that I stopped deciding the same things every day.

Here is the structure. Copy it into whatever tool you use.

## 1. Channels, decided once

Write down every channel and what it is for. One line each.

| Channel | What it is for | Days |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (me) | Daily lessons from real work | Every day |
| Company page | Method and proof | Mon, Wed, Fri |
| X | Short ideas, three a day | Every day |
| Newsletter | Teach one thing in depth | Every other Tue |
| Company site articles | Long guides, four languages | Thu |
| Personal blog | Free resources like this one | Fri |

If a channel has no clear purpose, drop it.

## 2. Pillars, so you never ask what to post

Give each channel a short rotation of themes. Mine for LinkedIn: workflow, proof, build in public, point of view, story. Each day takes the next one.

## 3. An ideas library

Every time something in your work is worth sharing, save it with three fields: what happened, where it came from, and the angle. Mine fills itself during the week, so writing day starts from a full list instead of a blank page.

## 4. One approval column

Everything gets written in batches, with a proposed date, and lands in one column called To approve. Every day you do one thing: read, check the image, approve. That is the whole daily job.

## 5. Automate the boring part, keep the judgement

Posting at the right time, moving files, marking what went out: automate it. Deciding whether something is true and yours: never.

## The rule behind it

AI writes faster. Systems decide less. Build the system first, and AI becomes useful instead of noisy.

If you copy this, send me a picture of your version. I love seeing how people adapt it.
