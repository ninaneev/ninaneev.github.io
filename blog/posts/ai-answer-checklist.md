summary: Six boxes to tick before you act on anything an AI tells you about your business. Copy it, print it, share it with your team.

I use AI every day. It reads faster than I ever could. But I learned the hard way that a confident answer and a correct answer look exactly the same.

So before an AI answer is allowed to change a decision, it goes through six boxes. This is the exact list. It is free, copy it.

## The checklist

- [ ] **Every claim points to a record.** Which ticket, which review, which date. No record, no claim.
- [ ] **I opened three of those records myself.** Not the summary. The records.
- [ ] **I asked the same question twice, and the main finding held.** If it moves, it is not ready.
- [ ] **I asked what would prove it wrong.** A good answer knows its limits.
- [ ] **It has an owner and a date.** One name, one day by which someone decides.
- [ ] **A person signed it.** Someone who would defend it in a meeting.

## How to use it

Print it, or paste it at the top of any document where AI output feeds a decision: a monthly feedback summary, a churn report, a market scan. It takes minutes. Most of the time everything passes. The few times it does not are exactly the times it saves you.

## Why these six

AI tends to fail in four ways. It invents specific details, it changes its answer between runs, it repeats a mistake so consistently that it looks right, and it quietly drops the cases that do not fit. Each box above catches at least one of those. The last two make sure the answer actually turns into something someone does.
