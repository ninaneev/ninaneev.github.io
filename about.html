What I've built

Five open systems. One external brain.

Each system is a working product I run my own company on, released free under MIT, installable by anyone. Together they form the loop an external brain runs on.

  • Compass · a public signal scanner: reviews, forums, and market noise turned into a weekly, source cited brief
  • Canon · a local document brain: every answer cites the exact file and passage, or says not found
  • Continuum · persistent memory for Claude Code: sessions that open already briefed
  • Council · a decision engine: ten adversarial advisors, graded evidence, permanent decision records
  • Cadence · the operating rhythm: daily plans from real state, weekly reviews with a maintenance sweep

The commercial peak of the stack is Flowity AI, the managed external brain for B2B SaaS teams, and The Full Stack Install, the version of it you own yourself.

Research

Executive Signal Synthesis

My research formalizes what the systems practice: how fragmented business signals become structured, explainable, executive level decisions. ESS treats signal synthesis as its own layer between data processing and decision making, in four stages: ingestion, classification, clustering, synthesis. Published as an open preprint with a prototype implementation.

What drives me

Companies forget. People too. I refuse to accept that.

Every organization I've looked into already knew, somewhere in its documents and conversations, the answer to the question that was hurting it. The knowledge existed. The memory didn't. That gap, between what is known and what is used, is the most expensive thing nobody budgets for. Closing it is what gets me up in the morning.

I work in public because signal literacy is the whole point: if I can't read my own company honestly, I have no business reading yours. The numbers, the mistakes, and the decision records are all published as they happen.
